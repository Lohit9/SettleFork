How It Works: One Migration at a Time

1
Client #1 completes migration
RCB Industries migrates Prosys → Rootstock. 108 field mappings manually reviewed and approved by their team. Transformations written, validated, exported.
AI cost: ~$50 · Human time: ~40 hours · Total fields: 108
2
System extracts a template
On export, extractTemplate() captures every approved mapping as a field signature — table name + field name + data type + constraints. This is stored as a reusable template keyed to "Prosys → Rootstock".
Stored: 108 mapping entries + load order + transformation SQL
3
Client #2 starts same migration
Acme Manufacturing also uses Prosys, also moving to Rootstock. They upload their schemas. System detects: "We have a template for this pair."
4
Template auto-maps 70% of fields
applyTemplate() matches field signatures: exact match (same table + field + type) covers 75/108 fields instantly. The remaining 33 fields go to AI. The pre-mapped fields cost $0 in AI calls.
AI cost: ~$15 (33 fields only) · Human time: ~12 hours · Pre-mapped: 75 fields FREE
5
Client #2 finishes — template gets smarter
mergeIntoTemplate() folds their approved mappings back in. The template now has 108 + 33 = 141 mapping entries. Fields that matched keep their confidence. New fields get added. Overrides replace bad suggestions.
6
Self-correction: bad mappings die automatically
If Client #2's human overrode a template mapping, the overrideCount increments. When overrides exceed reuses, the template replaces the bad mapping with the human's correction. No manual curation needed.
7
Client #10: 95% pre-mapped, near-zero AI cost
By the tenth Prosys → Rootstock migration, the template covers almost everything. AI runs only on the rare custom fields unique to that client. The migration that used to take a Deloitte team 4 months now takes an afternoon.
AI cost: ~$3 · Human time: ~2 hours · Template coverage: 95%

The Self-Correcting Secret

Every template mapping tracks two counters: reuseCount (how many times it was accepted as-is) and overrideCount (how many times a human changed it).

When overrides exceed reuses, the mapping was wrong — the template automatically replaces it with what humans keep choosing instead. No manual curation, no stale templates. The system learns from corrections naturally.

This means Settle's templates get more accurate over time without anyone maintaining them. A competitor who copies the template idea but skips override tracking will have stale templates within months.
